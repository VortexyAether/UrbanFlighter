\begin{abstract}
Urban air routing needs a simulator in which buildings, local wind, drag, sensing, and energy interact, without leaking privileged flow fields into a learning agent.
Urban Flighter is an open-source urban drone simulator that couples real OpenStreetMap geometry and forecast-model inlet wind with a browser cockpit and a headless research environment.
The contribution is not a new Navier--Stokes solver.
It is an explicit contract: cheap urban-flow approximations and quadratic air-relative drag may drive dynamics, energy, and metrics, while a policy may observe only mission context, the known inlet, onboard relative-air estimates, and deterministic geometry LiDAR.
We document the flyable 2D / 3D Lite loop, the potential-flow CFD-lite field with wall damping and empirical wake correction, a momentum-theory induced plus quadratic parasite power model, and UrbanFlow Gym, which is RL-ready but \textbf{not trained}.
Two checkable studies are reported.
A still-air / headwind / tailwind integration of the shared drag core shows stick-off drift toward local wind and a higher energy cost in headwind than tailwind under the same thrust.
An offline Gym fixture shows that unconstrained goal-seeking collides, geometry-safe A* succeeds, and an inlet-aware heuristic is \emph{not} automatically energy-optimal.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Small uncrewed aircraft in cities feel two things that game physics usually erases: buildings that must not be hit, and air that is not still.
A tailwind lowers relative airspeed; a headwind raises it; a wake behind a slab can do both in one block.
Those effects change both the trajectory a vehicle can hold and the energy it burns~\cite{hoffmann2007quadrotor}.

Existing aerial simulators often optimize photorealism or vehicle dynamics~\cite{shah2018airsim,guerra2019flightgoggles}.
Standalone CFD packages optimize field accuracy and are not interactive flight products.
Urban Flighter sits between them.
It loads real OpenStreetMap (OSM) footprints when a city is requested~\cite{haklay2008osm}, computes a cheap hidden flow field, flies a metre-scale quadrotor under quadratic air-relative drag, and exposes a Gymnasium-shaped interface~\cite{brockman2016gym} whose actor channel is forbidden from seeing the grid.

This paper is a system / software preprint, not a turbulence-model paper and not a trained-policy paper.
Every quantitative number below is produced by committed scripts.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item An honest full-stack urban-air cockpit: OSM geometry, CFD-lite hidden flow, 120\,Hz flyable 2D and 3D motion, and simulator LiDAR maps that are labelled as odometry, not SLAM.
  \item A shared quadratic air-relative drag core used by flyable motion and energy accounting, with momentum-theory induced power. Stick-off equilibrium is local wind, not still air.
  \item UrbanFlow Gym: a NumPy environment with leakage guards, live-scenario binding, and three deterministic baselines. Status: \textbf{NOT TRAINED}.
  \item Two reproducible studies (drag integration; offline Gym fixture) whose qualitative outcomes are stated in advance and not rewritten after the run.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}

AirSim provides Unreal-based visual and physical simulation for autonomous vehicles, including a quadrotor, and compares software components with real flights~\cite{shah2018airsim}.
FlightGoggles emphasizes photorealistic exteroceptive sensing and can keep complex vehicle dynamics outside the renderer~\cite{guerra2019flightgoggles}.
Urban Flighter does the opposite of photogrammetry-first design: the primary object is a research contract over urban wind, drag, and observations, rendered in a browser rather than Unreal or Unity.

Hoffmann et al.\ showed that even moderate airspeed changes quadrotor thrust and attitude, so hover-only models fail outdoors~\cite{hoffmann2007quadrotor}.
We take that lesson at a coarser grain: parasite drag and induced power depend on $\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}}=\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{g}}-\mathbf{w}$, not on ground speed alone.
The model is not blade-element theory and is not a claim to match STARMAC flight data.

OpenStreetMap supplies the building layer~\cite{haklay2008osm}.
The Gym-style $\mathrm{reset}/\mathrm{step}$ shape follows the OpenAI Gym interface~\cite{brockman2016gym}.
Gymnasium / Stable-Baselines3 / Torch remain optional extras; the default UrbanFlow core is NumPy-only.

\paragraph{Backend.}
\texttt{/flow-fields/2d} returns OSM polygons, an Open-Meteo (or labelled fallback) inlet, and a streamfunction / potential-flow-ish grid with wall damping and an empirical wake correction (``CFD-lite B'').
A successful non-empty response is registered as an immutable snapshot \texttt{urbanflow.live\_scenario.v1}.

\paragraph{Frontend.}
Three public modes share the same flyable horizontal grid:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{2D}: north-up canvas, 120\,Hz, swept-circle clearance, 180-ray scan.
  \item \textbf{3D Lite}: 0.58\,m visual quad, 1.25\,m horizontal / 2\,m roof research envelope, building and ground returns.
  \item \textbf{True 3D Wind}: the same flyable city plus a bundled voxel potential-flow $u/v/w$ overlay. Overlay $\neq$ flight dynamics.
\end{itemize}
Presentation trees, lamps, and road paint never enter collision, LiDAR, Gym, reward, or scenario hashes.

\section{Hidden flow}
\label{sec:flow}

A 2D slice solves a discrete Laplace problem on a masked grid and recovers a velocity field from the potential or streamfunction.
Inlet / far-field velocity is prescribed; building cells are solid.
This is cheap and produces acceleration around corners (Fig.~\ref{fig:flow}).
It cannot represent viscous separation, vortex shedding, or turbulence.
Empirical wall damping and downstream wake patches are therefore added for drone-risk texture and must be named as such.

A voxel potential-flow field can supply $u,v,w$ streamlines for visualization.
The flyable aircraft still samples the live horizontal CFD-lite grid.
No Navier--Stokes residual is claimed.

\section{Quadratic air-relative drag}
\label{sec:drag}

Let $\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{g}}$ be ground velocity and $\mathbf{w}$ the hidden local wind.
Relative air velocity is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}} = \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{g}} - \mathbf{w}.
\end{equation}
Parasite force and power use the standard quadratic law
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{p}} = -\tfrac12\rho C_d A \lVert\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}}\rVert \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}},
  \qquad
  P_{\mathrm{p}} = \tfrac12\rho C_d A \lVert\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}}\rVert^3.
\end{equation}
The flyable integrators apply an implicit step that is unconditionally stable at the 120\,Hz cockpit rate and at the larger diagnostic steps used in collision tests:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}}^{+}
  =
  \frac{\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}}}{1+\Delta t\bigl(k\lVert\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}}\rVert+c\bigr)},
  \qquad
  k=\frac{\rho C_d A}{2m}.
\end{equation}
With thrust off, the only equilibrium is $\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{g}}=\mathbf{w}$.
That is the difference from the previous linear ground-velocity damper, which stopped the vehicle in still air even inside a wind field.

Induced power uses momentum theory, not blade element:
\begin{equation}
  v_h=\sqrt{\frac{W}{2\rho A_{\mathrm{disk}}}},
  \qquad
  P_{\mathrm{i}}
  =
  \frac{\kappa W v_h}{\sqrt{1+(\lVert\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}}\rVert/v_h)^2}}.
\end{equation}
Total electrical-equivalent power is avionics + sensors + $P_{\mathrm{p}}$ + $P_{\mathrm{i}}$ + climb $W\max(v_z,0)$.
Default constants: $m=2.5$\,kg, $\rho=1.225$\,kg/m$^3$, $C_d=1.05$, $A=0.18$\,m$^2$, four 0.15\,m propellers, $\kappa=1.15$.
Then $k=0.0463$\,m$^{-1}$.

UrbanFlow Gym still uses a linear wind-drag gain by default so the published RL contract does not silently change.
The quadratic core is shared by the cockpit, a Python module, and a Unity C\# port of the same formulas.
The Unity file is not a scene rewrite.

\section{Sensing and the actor contract}

Frontend LiDAR prototypes use a frozen five-value-per-ray layout
$(d_x,d_y,d_z,\hat r,h)$:
local direction, normalized range, and hit flag.
3D default: 600 Fibonacci-sphere samples, 120\,m.
2D default: 180 rays, 180\,m.
Ground is a real target: downward rays intersect $y=0$ and take the nearer of ground and buildings.

UrbanFlow Gym uses a 33-scalar actor observation: own motion, goal context, fixed-angle geometry LiDAR, known inlet, previous action, and an onboard relative-air estimate.
Automated leakage guards reject full grids, exact wakes, future samples, and provider objects.
A privileged critic helper exists and is never returned by \texttt{reset}/\texttt{step}.

\section{UrbanFlow Gym}

The core is NumPy-only.
Action is a bounded 2D local-guidance command, not motor RPM.
Three deterministic baselines share only public mission context:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Direct goal (no obstacle planner).
  \item Geometry-safe A* + waypoint tracking.
  \item Inlet-aware A* with known-inlet feed-forward. Still not privileged-grid access.
\end{enumerate}
Live evaluation binds to the exact UI-registered OSM scenario.
Section~\ref{sec:gymcase} uses the synthetic rectangular fixture so the committed study needs no network.

\paragraph{Still-air power split.}
Figure~\ref{fig:power} integrates the analytic power model over $\lvert\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}}\rvert\in[0,18]$\,m/s.
Hover total power is $362$\,W.
The minimum is $350$\,W at $5.2$\,m/s.
At $11$\,m/s the total is $427$\,W: induced has fallen, parasite has risen.
This is the expected U-shape of a coarse momentum-plus-parasite model, not a fitted motor map.

\paragraph{Same thrust, three winds.}
A constant $8$\,m/s$^2$ body thrust is integrated for $8$\,s at $\Delta t=1/120$\,s (Fig.~\ref{fig:wind}, left).
Final ground speeds: tailwind $+6$\,m/s $\rightarrow$ $16.4$\,m/s; still air $\rightarrow$ $10.4$\,m/s; headwind $-6$\,m/s $\rightarrow$ $4.4$\,m/s.
Integrated energy: $3116$\,J, $3151$\,J, $3203$\,J.
Headwind is slower and costlier.
The energy gap is modest because the run is short and induced still dominates at these speeds; the speed gap is not.

\paragraph{Stick-off.}
Zero thrust in an $8$\,m/s wind reaches $7.28$\,m/s ground speed in $6$\,s (Fig.~\ref{fig:wind}, right).
The vehicle is drifting with the air, not parking in the world frame.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item CFD-lite and voxel potential flow are not LES/RANS/DNS.
  \item True 3D Wind is a visualization overlay.
  \item Flyable motion is a damped point-mass with quadratic air drag, not rotor flapping or blade-element moments~\cite{hoffmann2007quadrotor}.
  \item LiDAR and rolling maps are simulator rays plus odometry, not hardware SLAM.
  \item No PPO/SAC training is shipped.
  \item Live OSM/weather paths need network; the committed Gym showcase does not.
  \item Presentation geometry never enters physics.
  \item Gym still uses linear wind drag by default.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

Urban Flighter is an open urban-air research cockpit: real cities when requested, cheap hidden flow, quadratic air-relative drag in the flyable loop, and a deliberately narrow actor contract.
The public numbers are intentionally small and checkable.
That is the point.
