\begin{abstract}
Urban drone routing is not a still-air waypoint problem.
Buildings change the local wind, and quadratic air-relative drag changes both the trajectory a vehicle can hold and the energy it burns.
Most aerial simulators optimize photorealism or vehicle dynamics; most CFD packages optimize field accuracy and are not interactive flight products.
Urban Flighter sits between them.
It loads real OpenStreetMap footprints for an arbitrary city, pulls a forecast-model inlet velocity from Open-Meteo, computes a cheap hidden 2D flow (potential flow with near-wall damping and an empirical wake), and flies a metre-scale quadrotor under a shared quadratic air-relative drag core.
A browser cockpit exposes 2D and 3D Lite flight, deterministic LiDAR-style returns, and a simulated range--Doppler radar proxy.
A headless Gymnasium-shaped environment, UrbanFlow Gym, uses the same geometry, inlet, and hidden field, but returns only a 49-dimensional actor observation: odometry, an inlet-only relative-air estimate, goal context, 16-ray geometry LiDAR, and an 8-beam radar proxy.
The actor never receives the velocity grid.
This paper is a system preprint, not a Navier--Stokes paper and not a trained-policy paper.
Two checkable studies are reported: a still-air / headwind / tailwind integration of the drag core, and an offline Gym fixture in which unconstrained goal-seeking collides while geometry-safe A* succeeds.
An inlet-aware heuristic is \emph{not} automatically energy-optimal.
No PPO or SAC weights are shipped.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A quadrotor flying between real buildings feels two things that game physics usually erases: solids that must not be hit, and air that is not still.
A tailwind lowers relative airspeed; a headwind raises it; a wake behind a slab can do both in one block~\cite{hoffmann2007quadrotor,ware2016energy}.
Those effects change both the path a vehicle can hold and the energy it burns.

The practical research question is narrower than ``solve urban CFD.''
Given only (i)~the building geometry of the place you are in and (ii)~a forecast inlet velocity $U_\infty$, can a planner or controller choose the next route or local command so as to reduce drag and avoid collisions?
That is a sensing-and-control problem, not a grid-access problem.
A learning agent that sees the full hidden flow is solving a different, easier task than a vehicle that will later fly with LiDAR, a coarse radar, and a weather forecast.

Existing stacks split the wrong way.
AirSim and FlightGoggles emphasize visual fidelity and vehicle dynamics~\cite{shah2018airsim,guerra2019flightgoggles}.
CARLA and PX4 SITL are mature for ground vehicles or firmware-in-the-loop flight, not urban wind-aware energy~\cite{dosovitskiy2017carla,meier2015px4}.
Full CFD remains the right tool for pedestrian-wind studies and is the wrong tool for an interactive research cockpit~\cite{blocken2014cfd,tominaga2008aij}.
Urban Flighter is an open-source compromise: real geometry, real inlet, cheap hidden flow, honest sensors, and an RL contract that forbids grid leakage.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A full-stack urban-air cockpit that, for any requested city, loads OSM footprints~\cite{haklay2008osm,boeing2017osmnx} and a forecast-model inlet, computes a toy CFD-lite field in interactive time, and flies a 120\,Hz 2D / 3D Lite vehicle under quadratic air-relative drag.
  \item A shared drag-and-power core used by the browser, a Python module, and a formula-only Unity port. Stick-off equilibrium is local wind, not still air.
  \item Deterministic geometry LiDAR and a simulated range--Doppler radar proxy. Rolling maps are labelled as simulator odometry, not SLAM or RF hardware~\cite{patole2017fmcw}.
  \item UrbanFlow Gym: a NumPy environment with a 49-D actor observation, leakage guards, live-OSM scenario binding, three deterministic baselines, and optional PPO/SAC extras~\cite{towers2024gymnasium,schulman2017ppo,haarnoja2018sac,raffin2021sb3}. Status: \textbf{NOT TRAINED}.
  \item Two reproducible studies whose qualitative outcomes are stated in advance.
\end{enumerate}

This paper does not claim a new turbulence model, a hardware radar, or a learned energy-optimal policy.
Those are future journal targets; the present artifact is the simulator and the contract.

\section{Related work}

AirSim couples Unreal visuals with vehicle dynamics and compares software components to real flights~\cite{shah2018airsim}.
FlightGoggles keeps complex dynamics outside a photorealistic renderer~\cite{guerra2019flightgoggles}.
Urban Flighter does the opposite of photogrammetry-first design: the primary object is a research contract over urban wind, drag, and observations, rendered in a browser.

Hoffmann et al.\ showed that even moderate airspeed changes quadrotor thrust and attitude, so hover-only models fail outdoors~\cite{hoffmann2007quadrotor}.
Ware and Roy studied wind-field estimation and exploitation in the urban canopy~\cite{ware2016energy}.
We take that lesson at a coarser grain: parasite drag and induced power depend on $\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}}=\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{g}}-\mathbf{w}$, not on ground speed alone~\cite{leishman2006principles}.
The model is momentum theory plus a quadratic parasite term, not blade-element theory, and is not a claim to match STARMAC flight data.

OpenStreetMap and OSMnx supply the building layer~\cite{haklay2008osm,boeing2017osmnx}.
Open-Meteo supplies the 10\,m forecast-model current wind used as $U_\infty$.
The Gym-style $\mathrm{reset}/\mathrm{step}$ shape follows OpenAI Gym / Gymnasium~\cite{brockman2016gym,towers2024gymnasium}.
Stable-Baselines3 PPO and SAC are optional extras~\cite{schulman2017ppo,haarnoja2018sac,raffin2021sb3}.
The default UrbanFlow core is NumPy-only.

Commercial driving-and-drone simulators (for example MORAI-class stacks) target certification-style sensor realism.
Urban Flighter is intentionally smaller: one honest research loop from real geometry and a real inlet to drag-aware flight and an RL interface.

\section{Real geometry and real inlet}

Buildings come from OSMnx \texttt{features\_from\_point} around a user-selected latitude and longitude~\cite{boeing2017osmnx}.
Footprints are projected to a local east/north metre frame.
Height uses \texttt{osm:height} if present, otherwise \texttt{building:levels}$\times 3.5$\,m, otherwise a labelled 10\,m default.
The default demo location is Inha / Incheon ($37.451448^\circ$N, $126.651542^\circ$E).
Any other city is a picker click, not a code change.

The inlet is the Open-Meteo forecast-model current 10\,m wind, stored as a 2D velocity $(u_x,u_y)$ and as meteorological direction-from-north.
If the fetch fails or returns an unusable vector, the payload is a deterministic 5\,m/s north-origin fallback with explicit \texttt{source} and \texttt{fallback} fields.
There is no silent fake sensor.

This inlet is the \emph{known} wind: it appears on the HUD and in the actor observation.
Building-scale local flow is not known to the actor.

\section{Hidden toy CFD}
\label{sec:flow}

A 2D slice solves a discrete Laplace problem on a masked grid and recovers velocity from the streamfunction~\cite{anderson2017fundamentals}.
Inlet / far-field velocity is prescribed; building cells are solid.
The production solver (CFD-lite B) then applies two cheap, named corrections that do not change the Jacobi budget:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Near-wall-only damping.} Far-field cells keep the potential-flow solution; a short exponential blend slows fluid only near the mask.
  \item \textbf{Empirical wake deficit.} Downstream of each footprint, a Gaussian shelter multiplies speed. This is not vortex shedding.
\end{enumerate}
Sampling is mask-aware bilinear: solid corners do not bleed into fluid interpolants.
That removes the most common ``streamline through a building'' artifact without a finer grid.

This is cheap and produces acceleration around corners (Fig.~\ref{fig:flow}).
It cannot represent viscous separation, vortex shedding, or turbulence~\cite{blocken2014cfd}.
No Navier--Stokes residual is claimed.
A voxel potential-flow field can supply $u,v,w$ streamlines for visualization; the flyable aircraft still samples the live horizontal CFD-lite grid.

\section{Quadratic air-relative drag}
\label{sec:drag}

Let $\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{g}}$ be ground velocity and $\mathbf{w}$ the hidden local wind.
Relative air velocity is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}} = \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{g}} - \mathbf{w}.
\end{equation}
Parasite force and power use the standard quadratic law
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{p}} = -\tfrac12\rho C_d A \lVert\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}}\rVert \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}},
  \qquad
  P_{\mathrm{p}} = \tfrac12\rho C_d A \lVert\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}}\rVert^3.
\end{equation}
The flyable integrators apply an implicit step that is unconditionally stable at the 120\,Hz cockpit rate and at the larger Gym step:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}}^{+}
  =
  \frac{\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}}}{1+\Delta t\bigl(k\lVert\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}}\rVert+c\bigr)},
  \qquad
  k=\frac{\rho C_d A}{2m}.
\end{equation}
With thrust off, the only equilibrium is $\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{g}}=\mathbf{w}$.

Induced power uses momentum theory, not blade element~\cite{leishman2006principles}:
\begin{equation}
  v_h=\sqrt{\frac{W}{2\rho A_{\mathrm{disk}}}},
  \qquad
  P_{\mathrm{i}}
  =
  \frac{\kappa W v_h}{\sqrt{1+(\lVert\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}}\rVert/v_h)^2}}.
\end{equation}
Total electrical-equivalent power is avionics + sensors + $P_{\mathrm{p}}$ + $P_{\mathrm{i}}$ + climb $W\max(v_z,0)$.
Default constants: $m=2.5$\,kg, $\rho=1.225$\,kg/m$^3$, $C_d=1.05$, $A=0.18$\,m$^2$, four 0.15\,m propellers, $\kappa=1.15$.
Then $k=0.0463$\,m$^{-1}$.

UrbanFlow Gym now uses this same quadratic core for hidden dynamics.
Reward still includes an $\lVert\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}}\rVert^2$ energy proxy so that a later trained policy can be compared against the published baselines without a silent reward rewrite.
Episode metrics also record parasite energy in joules.

\section{Sensing}
\label{sec:sensing}

Frontend LiDAR prototypes use a frozen five-value-per-ray layout
$(d_x,d_y,d_z,\hat r,h)$:
local direction, normalized range, and hit flag.
3D default: 600 Fibonacci-sphere samples, 120\,m.
2D display default: 180 rays, 180\,m.
Ground is a real target: downward rays intersect $y=0$ and take the nearer of ground and buildings.

UrbanFlow Gym uses a coarser, actor-facing sensor set (Fig.~\ref{fig:obs}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item 16-ray geometry LiDAR, 35\,m, full azimuth, exact ray--polygon hits.
  \item 8-beam simulated radar, $\pm 60^\circ$ forward fan, 40\,m.
    Range is the same geometric intersection.
    Range-rate is $-\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{g}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{b}}$: a Doppler proxy for static buildings from own ground velocity~\cite{patole2017fmcw}.
\end{itemize}
The radar is not an FMCW waveform, has no RCS, no CFAR, and no rain model.
It exists so that a later policy can use a second, narrower exteroceptive channel without inventing RF physics.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{figures/fig_observation.png}
  \caption{Actor observation versus hidden state.
  Relative air in the actor vector is $\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{g}}-U_\infty$, not $\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{g}}-\mathbf{w}(\mathbf{x})$.}

Rolling sensor maps accumulate display-only scan history with simulator odometry.
They are not hardware-qualified LiDAR, loop closure, or production SLAM.

\section{UrbanFlow Gym}
\label{sec:gym}

The core is NumPy-only.
Action is a bounded 2D local-guidance command, not motor RPM.
The 49-scalar actor observation is: own motion, an inlet-only relative-air estimate, known inlet, goal context, LiDAR, radar, and the previous action.
Automated leakage guards reject full grids, exact wakes, future samples, and provider objects.
A privileged critic helper exists and is never returned by \texttt{reset}/\texttt{step}.

Three deterministic baselines share only public mission context:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Direct goal (no obstacle planner).
  \item Geometry-safe A* + waypoint tracking.
  \item Inlet-aware A* with known-inlet feed-forward. Still not privileged-grid access.
\end{enumerate}

Live evaluation binds to the exact UI-registered OSM scenario.
Section~\ref{sec:gymcase} uses the synthetic rectangular fixture so the committed study needs no network.

Optional training extras install Gymnasium, Stable-Baselines3, and PyTorch~\cite{towers2024gymnasium,raffin2021sb3}.
The training entrypoint can run PPO or SAC on the fixture or on an exported live snapshot.
The evaluation entrypoint scores random, baseline, or checkpoint policies under the same observation contract.
No weights are bundled.
That is the foundation for a later journal paper on learned drag-minimizing control; it is not that paper.

\paragraph{Still-air power split.}
Figure~\ref{fig:power} integrates the analytic power model over $\lvert\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}}\rvert\in[0,18]$\,m/s.
Hover total power is $362$\,W.
The minimum is $350$\,W at $5.2$\,m/s.
At $11$\,m/s the total is $427$\,W: induced has fallen, parasite has risen.
This is the expected U-shape of a coarse momentum-plus-parasite model, not a fitted motor map.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.78\linewidth]{figures/fig_power_curve.png}
  \caption{Still-air power versus relative airspeed.
  Induced falls, parasite rises.
  Not blade-element theory.}
  \label{fig:power}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Same thrust, three winds.}
A constant $8$\,m/s$^2$ body thrust is integrated for $8$\,s at $\Delta t=1/120$\,s (Fig.~\ref{fig:wind}, left).
Final ground speeds: tailwind $+6$\,m/s $\rightarrow$ $16.4$\,m/s; still air $\rightarrow$ $10.4$\,m/s; headwind $-6$\,m/s $\rightarrow$ $4.4$\,m/s.
Integrated energy: $3116$\,J, $3151$\,J, $3203$\,J.
Headwind is slower and costlier.
The energy gap is modest because the run is short and induced still dominates at these speeds; the speed gap is not.

\paragraph{Stick-off.}
Zero thrust in an $8$\,m/s wind reaches $7.28$\,m/s ground speed in $6$\,s (Fig.~\ref{fig:wind}, right).
The vehicle is drifting with the air, not parking in the world frame.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item CFD-lite and voxel potential flow are not LES/RANS/DNS~\cite{blocken2014cfd,tominaga2008aij}.
  \item True 3D Wind is a visualization overlay over a bundled Gangnam field; it does not follow the selected city.
  \item Flyable motion is a damped point-mass with quadratic air drag, not rotor flapping or blade-element moments~\cite{hoffmann2007quadrotor}.
  \item LiDAR and rolling maps are simulator rays plus odometry, not hardware SLAM.
  \item Radar is a geometric range--Doppler proxy, not an electromagnetic sensor~\cite{patole2017fmcw}.
  \item Browser display LiDAR (180 / 600 rays) is denser than the Gym actor set (16 + 8).
  \item No PPO/SAC training is shipped.
  \item Live OSM/weather paths need network; the committed Gym showcase does not.
  \item Presentation geometry never enters physics.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

Urban Flighter is an open urban-air research cockpit: real cities when requested, a real forecast inlet, cheap hidden flow, quadratic air-relative drag in the flyable loop, LiDAR and a simulated radar, and a deliberately narrow actor contract.
The public numbers are intentionally small and checkable.
A later paper can train PPO or SAC on that contract and ask whether geometry-plus-inlet is enough to reduce drag.
This paper only builds the machine that makes that question well-posed.
